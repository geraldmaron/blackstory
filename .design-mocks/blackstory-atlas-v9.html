01
Where the record is thickest

The archive is not evenly spread.

Coverage follows evidence, not headlines. The Mississippi Delta, the Carolina low country, and the District carry the deepest documentation in this release because that is where the paper survived: church minutes, school registers, deeds, and Register nominations.

02
One record, up close

Every pin opens into evidence.

The A.G. Gaston Motel, Birmingham. Built in 1954 by a Black businessman, it became the campaign headquarters of the 1963 Birmingham movement. Room 30 was the war room. It is one pin, four blocks from the 16th Street Baptist Church, and it carries six citations you can read before you decide to trust it.

03
The Great Migration

Six million people, drawn as they moved.

Between 1910 and 1970 six million people left the South. The corridors were not random: rail lines and kin networks decided who landed in Chicago and who landed in Los Angeles. Records cluster at both ends of every line, which is why a Mississippi surname turns up in a Milwaukee school register.
