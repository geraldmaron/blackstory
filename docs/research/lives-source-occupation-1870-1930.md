# Occupation by race, by state, 1870-1930: what was published

**Status:** Research record, RESEARCH ONLY (no project files touched)
**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Question:** find a citable published source for the `work_based` class dimension of Lives Across
the Decades, 1870-1930, at state level where possible.

## Headline

**The Census Bureau published occupation crossed by race at state level for five of the seven
decades: 1890, 1900, 1910, 1920 and 1930.** All five are Tier A. All five cover every state and
territory, so each unlocks all six regions plus the national baseline.

**1870 and 1880 have no such table, at any geography.** The Ninth and Tenth Censuses crossed
occupation by sex, age and *nativity* only. This is not a gap in what survives or in what has been
digitized. Those two censuses never tabulated occupation against color.

Every source below was opened and the named table looked at, page by page. Where I read a page
range from a volume's own contents rather than opening each page, I say so.

## Tier A sources, state level

### 1. 1890 — Special Census Report on the Occupations of the Population of the United States at the Eleventh Census: 1890

- **Citation:** Department of the Interior, Census Office, *Special Census Report on the
  Occupations of the Population of the United States at the Eleventh Census: 1890*, prepared by
  William C. Hunt under Carroll D. Wright, Washington: Government Printing Office, 1896.
- **URL opened:**
  https://www2.census.gov/library/publications/decennial/1890/occupations/1890c3-01.pdf (pp. 1-64)
  and https://www2.census.gov/library/publications/decennial/1890/occupations/1890c3-02.pdf
  (pp. 65-127). Both are image scans with no text layer.
- **Tables carrying occupation by race:**
  - **Table 4, pp. 58-63** — "Total persons 10 years of age and over engaged in gainful occupations
    and in each class of occupations, classified by general nativity and color, by states and
    territories: 1890." One page per occupation class. (p. 59 opened and read, the
    "Agriculture, fisheries, and mining" panel.)
  - **Table 5, begins p. 64** — the same table with sex added: "…classified by sex, general
    nativity, and color, by states and territories: 1890." Same six class panels. (p. 64 opened
    and read.)
  - Table 3 (national, ends p. 73+) gives the full specified-occupation list by sex, general
    nativity and color for the United States. (p. 71 and p. 73 opened.)
- **Geography:** every state and territory, grouped under North Atlantic / South Atlantic /
  North Central / South Central / Western divisions, with a United States line. Complete.
- **Census year:** 1890 only.
- **Occupational categories published (the six panels):** All occupations; Agriculture, fisheries,
  and mining; Professional service; Domestic and personal service; Trade and transportation;
  Manufacturing and mechanical industries.
- **Race categories, in the volume's own words:** `Total white` | `Native white` (Total / Native
  parents / Foreign parents) | `Foreign white` | `Total colored (a)` | `Persons of negro descent`.
  Footnote (a) reads: "Persons of negro descent, Chinese, Japanese, and civilized Indians."
- **Tier A.** A Census Office publication of the Eleventh Census, printed by GPO.
- **Closes:** the whole 1890 cell, nation and all six regions. Black-only and all-non-white are
  both published, so the project can store `black` and `nonwhite` as distinct slices without
  deriving either.

> This also corrects the NHGIS map's line that "1890 has no occupation table at all." That is true
> of NHGIS. It is not true of the 1890 census, which published a 127-page occupations report plus
> a fuller set in Part II of the Report on Population.

### 3. 1910 — Thirteenth Census, Volume IV, Occupation Statistics

- **Citation:** Department of Commerce, Bureau of the Census, *Thirteenth Census of the United

- **URLs opened:**
  https://www2.census.gov/library/publications/decennial/1910/volume-4/volume-4-p1.pdf

  (General Tables VII-VIII). Printed p. 435 and p. 535 opened and read.
- **Table carrying occupation by race:**
  - **Table VII, pp. 434-534** — "Total males and females 10 years of age and over engaged in
    selected occupations, by age periods and color or race, nativity, and parentage, by states:
    1910." One block per state, alphabetically, Alabama p. 434 through Wyoming p. 533. The state
    list and page range come from the volume's own contents page, which I read; p. 435 (Alabama
    continued, Arizona) opened.
  - Table VI is the national companion: each specified occupation in each industry or service
    group by age, color or race, nativity and parentage.
- **Geography:** every state, alphabetically. Complete.
- **Census year:** 1910 only.
- **Occupational categories published:** selected occupations, listed under eight general-division
  headings — Agriculture, forestry, and animal husbandry; Extraction of minerals; Manufacturing
  and mechanical industries; Transportation; Trade; Public service (n.e.c.); Professional service;
  Domestic and personal service; Clerical occupations.
  **Caveat that matters for ingest:** the division headings in Table VII carry **no subtotal rows**.

  mixed parentage) | `Foreign-born white` | `Negro` | `Ind., Chi., Jap., and all other.`

- **Closes:** the whole 1910 cell, nation and all six regions, with the subtotal caveat above.

**Companion, Negro side only, 1910:** *Negro Population in the United States, 1790-1915* (below)
Tables 15, 17 and 18 publish complete Negro totals by state and a clean "All other" residual, so
the Negro column for 1910 can be built without the subtotal problem. The white column still comes
from Volume IV Table VII.

## Tier A sources, national only (use as cross-checks, and for the national baseline)

### 6. 1930 — Fifteenth Census, Volume V, General Report on Occupations, Chapter 3

- **Citation:** Bureau of the Census, *Fifteenth Census of the United States: 1930. Population,
  Volume V: General Report on Occupations*, Washington: GPO, 1933, Chapter 3, "Color and nativity
  of gainful workers."
- **URL opened:**
  https://www2.census.gov/library/publications/decennial/1930/population-volume-5/41129379v5ch3.pdf
  (p. 74 opened and read).
- **Table 2, p. 74** — "Gainful workers 10 years old and over, by color, nativity, sex, and general
  divisions of occupations, for the United States: **1930, 1920, and 1910**." Three census years,
  ten general divisions, in one table.
- **Race categories:** `Total` | `Native white` | `Foreign-born white` | `Negro` | `Other races`.
  Footnote to Table 2: "Other races comprises Mexicans, Indians, Chinese, Japanese, Filipinos,
  Hindus, Koreans, Hawaiians etc."
- **Geography:** United States only. Chapter 3's state tables (Tables 9-14, pp. 101 on) give
  *gainfully occupied rates* by color and nativity by state, not occupation by color by state.
- **Why it still matters:** it is the single best validation table in the whole search. Any
  state-level 1910/1920/1930 build should sum to these national figures, and the chapter's own text
  is what points to Volume IV for the state detail.
- **Definitional break it documents:** footnote 1 to Table 1 on the same page reads "In 1920 and in
  1910 Mexicans were included for the most part in the white population." In 1930 Mexicans were a
  separate race. So `white` in 1930 is not the same universe as `white` in 1920 and 1910, and a
  1920→1930 change in the white column crosses a definition. This must be on screen.

## DEAD ENDS

Each of these was opened and the actual table or table list looked at.

**1870 — Ninth Census, Volume I, *The Statistics of the Population of the United States* (1872).**
The occupation tables are Nos. XXVI-XXXII. The volume's own "Remarks on the Tables of Occupations"
(p. 659, opened) names them; the census.gov index lists them as: XXVI persons in each class of
occupations with sex, by states; XXVII number in each occupation, by states; XXVIII persons in each
class of occupations with age and sex and selected nationalities, by states; XXIX same for the
United States; XXX selected occupations with age and sex and selected nationalities, in each state;
XXXI-XXXII the same for cities. I opened Table XXVIII
(https://www2.census.gov/library/publications/decennial/1870/population/1870a-62.pdf, printed
p. 698): its only cross-cutting columns are age, sex, and thirteen **nativity** columns (United
States, Germany, Ireland, England and Wales, Scotland, Sweden/Norway/Denmark, France, Other north of
Europe, Italy, Other south of Europe, British America, China and Japan, Other and unknown).
**No race or color column exists in any 1870 occupation table.** I did not separately open the 1870
Compendium, which is a condensation of this volume.

**1880 — Tenth Census, Volume I, *Statistics of the Population* (1883).** The complete list of
occupation tables is printed on p. 701 (opened,
https://www2.census.gov/library/publications/decennial/1880/vol-01-population/1880_v1-01.pdf via
`1880_v1-20.pdf`): Tables XXIX-XXXVI, every one of them "with distinction of age and sex and of
nativity." **No race cross anywhere.** The volume's title-page phrase "with distinction of race,
sex, age, nativity, and occupations" describes the volume's dimensions collectively, not the
occupation tables.

**1880 — Compendium of the Tenth Census (1885).** Occupation tables CI-CV. I searched the full OCR
of the Compendium (Internet Archive `compendiumoftent02unit`) across the entire occupation-table
region: the strings "colored," "negro" and "race" **do not occur once**. Table CV is explicitly
"OCCUPATIONS, WITH AGE, SEX, AND NATIVITY."

**The Census Bureau's own retrospective confirms this.** The 1900 *Occupations at the Twelfth
Census* comparative chapter (contents p. v, opened) carries every 1880 comparison as "by sex" or
"by main classes … by states and territories, in 1880, 1890, and 1900." Its "Distribution by general
nativity and color" analyses go back only to **1890**. The Bureau itself could not compare
occupation by color to 1880.

**Historical Statistics of the United States, Bicentennial Edition (1975), Chapter D: Labor.**
Opened:
https://www2.census.gov/library/publications/1975/compendia/hist_stats_colonial-1970/hist_stats_colonial-1970p1-chD.pdf
(pp. 121-122, 127, 139, 140 read). Series D 11-25 (Labor Force Status of the Population, 1870-1970)
has no color breakdown. Series D 167-181 (Labor Force and Employment by Industry, 1800-1960) splits
the pre-1860 labor force into Free and Slave — a Lebergott estimate, not an occupation-by-race
table. Series D 182-232 (Major Occupation Group of the Experienced Civilian Labor Force, 1900-1970)
and D 233-682 (Detailed Occupation, 1900-1970) are **by sex only**. National throughout.
**HS 1975 adds nothing to the 1870-1930 class gap.**

**Henry Gannett, *Occupations of the Negroes* (1895).** Trustees of the John F. Slater Fund,
Occasional Papers No. 6, Baltimore, 1895; Gannett was of the United States Geological Survey.
Opened via https://archive.org/details/occupationsofneg00gann. It does carry a state-level table —
"the number of negroes in all occupations and in each of the five great groups of occupations by
sex and by states and territories" — but the text says it is "abstracted from the Census
publications." **This is exactly the trap the brief warned about:** it is a private-foundation
reprint of the same 1890 figures the Census Office published itself in 1896, so it is superseded by
source 1 above and should not be cited in its place. By publisher it would be Tier C.

**1930 Volume V, Chapter 3, state tables (Tables 9-14).** These give the *number and proportion
gainfully occupied* by color and nativity for divisions and states, and per cent distribution of
the occupied population by color. They do **not** cross occupation by color at state level. Easy to
mistake for the table you want; it isn't.

**1920 Volume IV, Chapter III, state tables (Tables 6-10).** Same shape, same trap: total gainful
workers by color or race, nativity and parentage, by divisions and states (Table 10, p. 364), but
no occupation dimension.

**IPUMS NHGIS.** Already established before this search and not re-litigated here.

## RECOMMENDATION

**Transcribe the decennial state occupation tables directly: 1890 Table 4/5, 1900 Table 34, 1910
Volume IV Table VII, 1920 Volume IV Chapter VII Table 1, and 1930 Volume IV state Table 11.** That
is one published Census Bureau table per decade, all Tier A, all state level, all storable as
`observed` under the method exactly as written, with regions `derived` by the existing state-sum
rule. No methodology change is needed and no new status is needed.

**Coverage: five of the seven `work_based` decades, and for each of those five, all seven areas
(six regions plus the national baseline).** In cell terms that is 35 of the 49 region-decade cells
of the class dimension, versus 0 today.

**Sequencing.** Do **1930 and 1920 first.** Both print general-division subtotal rows, so a coarse
lower/middle/upper bucketing can be read straight off the page without classifying individual
occupations, and 1930 additionally prints the farm-tenure split the method asks for. Then **1900
and 1890**, which publish complete class totals in five or six columns per state — the least
transcription work of all five, and the only two decades that publish the Black-only and
all-non-white slices side by side. Do **1910 last**: it is the only one of the five without
division subtotals, so it needs an occupation-by-occupation classification with a residual, and its
Negro column is easier to take from the 1918 volume's Tables 17 and 18 than from Volume IV.

**Validate every decade against the national totals** in 1930 Volume V Chapter 3 Table 2 (p. 74),
which gives 1910, 1920 and 1930 by the ten general divisions and by color in one place. For 1900 and
1890, validate against each volume's own "The United States" line.

**1870 and 1880 stay empty, and the surface should say why.** The honest sentence is that these two
censuses counted occupation and counted race but never published the two together — occupation was
crossed by sex, age and country of birth only. That is a fact about what the government chose to
tabulate seven and seventeen years after emancipation, and the project treats gaps as content, so
it belongs in the decade's "What the count could see" note rather than being papered over. The only
way to fill them is a microdata tabulation, which is Tier C and against the current method.

## Universe notes that must travel with the figures

- Every one of these tables counts **persons 10 years old and over** who were gainfully occupied.
  That is not the same universe as the modern labor force and it is not the same as the
  population, so class shares are shares of workers, not of people.
- 1890 also published a 16-and-over comparison series for 1870 and 1880; do not mix the bases.
- 1910 and 1920 state tables are **selected** occupations (1910: no division subtotals; 1920: the
  more important of 572, sometimes shown as a main group). 1930's Table 11 is likewise "selected
  occupations," but its division subtotals are complete and reconcile to the state's `MALES` and
  `FEMALES` totals — I checked Alabama against the 1935 volume and against Table 9 on p. 117.
- The 1918 and 1935 volumes' state tables end in an `All other` row, so those two are complete for
  the Negro column without any residual arithmetic.
