# Neo profile: how durable personas are actually built

**Status:** research memo backing Part I and Part VIII of
`docs/content/neo-voice.md`. Not binding on its own. Compiled 2026-08-21 from
fetched sources (URLs inline).

## Bottom line

Nothing in professional practice treats a persona as a personality
description. What holds voice steady across many writers and years is a small
set of *constraints* (prohibitions, ranked values, calibration samples) plus a
routine that re-applies them at edit time. Backstory is the least load-bearing
part of a bible; the never-list and the tie-break rules are the most.

## Writers'-room character bibles

The show bible's function is continuity-across-writers: character-specific
speech patterns, vocabulary, and running continuity of everything aired, so a
staff of five produces one character
(https://scriptation.com/blog/tv-show-bible-and-character-bibles-guide/,
https://screencraft.org/blog/10-steps-to-developing-your-tv-show-bible/). The
*Frasier* bible is the cited exemplar of the maintained kind: updated with
everything established on air so writers check rather than invent.

The most instructive documented artifact is the Star Trek: TNG
Writers'/Directors' Guide (1987, scan:
https://archive.org/details/sttng-writer-directors-guide-1987-03). Its most
consequential clause, "the Roddenberry Box" (Starfleet characters do not have
petty interpersonal conflict), survived thirty years, multiple showrunners,
and hundreds of scripts, and every writer could apply it without knowing any
character's childhood (https://forgottentrek.com/the-next-generation/writing-the-next-generation/).
**The single most durable element of that bible was a prohibition, not a
biography.** Prohibitions are portable; backstory is not.

Want vs. need (Truby, Yorke): the conscious external goal the audience rides
along with vs. the internal pressure that makes judgments feel motivated
(https://www.johnyorkestory.com/2021/10/john-yorke-on-internal-desire/). For a
narrator persona: the want is what it's overtly doing to the reader; the need
is why its choices are predictable.

## Brand persona frameworks

Aaker (1997): five personality dimensions (Sincerity, Excitement, Competence,
Sophistication, Ruggedness); practice picks one primary, one secondary
(https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/002224379703400304). Use as
naming vocabulary, not diagnostic; cross-cultural stability is questioned.

Mark & Pearson, *The Hero and the Outlaw* (2001): twelve Jungian archetypes;
warns against *possession* by the archetype's negative potential
(https://thebrandarchetypes.com/history/pearson-mark.html). For a
truth-telling narrator: **Sage primary** (truth, evidence-led, restrained;
exemplars include the BBC and The Economist), with the Caregiver as a
strictly subordinate secondary. Possession risks: Sage → pedantry,
detachment, condescension, withholding judgment forever; Caregiver →
softening hard facts to spare the reader; Hero → needing an enemy, history as
scoreboard (https://scottjeffrey.com/12-brand-archetype-wheel/). The profile
must state which archetype wins under conflict or the persona drifts.

Longevity case: Betty Crocker, created 1921, held stable across decades by a
department, a signature, and standards, not one author; radio-voiced by one
performer for twenty years; Fortune 1945 called her personality a major
corporate asset (https://www.generalmills.com/news/stories/the-women-who-built-betty-crocker,
https://fortune.com/article/general-mills-of-minneapolis-betty-crocker/).

## Narrative identity as construction scaffold

McAdams: identity is an internalized, evolving narrative; his interview
protocol (chapters, then key scenes: high point, low point, turning point,
earliest memory) is directly reusable as a persona-construction template
(https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1037/1089-2680.5.2.100). Formative
scenes make judgments predictable when each scene derives a behavior:
"therefore, on X, the narrator reliably does Y."

Warning: a narrator scaffolded on the redemptive form will reliably reach for
uplift at the end of every hard story
(https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0963721413475622). If that is not
wanted, the profile must say so explicitly, because the scaffold supplies it
silently. (Neo's Part I arc note exists because of this finding.)

## Editorial narrators that read as a person

- **The Economist**: no bylines; voice enforced by house style plus a
  mandatory collective edit pass before publication
  (https://talkingbiznews.com/they-talk-biz-news/the-pros-and-cons-of-anonymity-at-the-economist/,
  https://medium.com/severe-contest/we-are-anonymous-87a82714ee5). Voice is
  preserved by a gate, not by asking writers to be consistent.
- **Ira Glass / This American Life**: the anecdote / moment-of-reflection
  alternation as a structural voice signature; the "taste gap" as the
  argument for an external standard new writers are measured against
  (https://medium.com/@DanlWebster/ira-glass-on-storytelling-1-of-4-rough-transcript-9bb2dc8e27f7).
- **Radiolab**: the persona survived the founder's 2022 departure because it
  lived in the form (audible doubt, thinking-in-public), not the throat
  (https://current.org/2022/01/jad-abumrad-steps-down-from-radiolab/).
- **Attenborough**: scripts two weeks out, argued word by word with a single
  arbiter, recorded against picture; long considered sentences building
  through subordinate clauses (https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/working-with-david-attenborough-our-planet-2).
- **Mailchimp**: the cleanest operational voice/tone distinction: voice
  constant, tone varies with the reader's emotional state; guide is public
  and versioned (https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/).

## Operational tests

- **Name-blind attribution**: black out the narrator and sort persona
  paragraphs from generic ones; failure means the persona is decorative
  (https://screencraft.org/2018/10/25/5-simple-tests-screenplays-need-pass).
- **Never-list**: a small number of absolute prohibitions any stranger can
  apply, checked at edit; include at least one that costs something.
- **Paired calibration samples**: matched near-identical sentences, one in
  voice and one just off, with the why; the only part of a guide that
  transfers reliably.
- **The collective edit gate**: no piece publishes without a pass by someone
  whose job is the voice.
- **Conflict cases**: hard scenarios with the persona's correct answer
  recorded, so the value hierarchy is exercised, not declared.

## The schema (implemented in neo-voice.md Part I)

1. Identity core (archetype primary/secondary with subordination rule; want
   and need). 2. Formation (four scenes, each deriving a behavior; explicit
   arc note). 3. Values, strictly ranked (ties are defects). 4. Decision
   heuristics under conflict. 5. Speech fingerprint (Parts I and IV).
6. Never-list. 7. Tone map. 8. Calibration set (Part VI). 9. Maintenance and
QA (Part VIII).

Evidence-quality note: the TNG guide and Economist style guide are public but
their internal wording was not extractable in this pass; the writers'-room
and fingerprint sections rest partly on trade-craft consensus. The McAdams,
Betty Crocker, Attenborough, and Radiolab material is primary- or
near-primary-sourced.
