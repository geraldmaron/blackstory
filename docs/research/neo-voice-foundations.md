# Neo voice: research foundations

**Status:** research memo backing `docs/content/neo-voice.md`. Not binding on its
own; the voice bible is the binding document. This memo records where the rules
came from so future edits argue with evidence, not preference.
Compiled 2026-08-21 from fetched sources (URLs inline). Findings are separated
into *verified with source*, *craft consensus* (widely held among practitioners,
not empirically tested), and *inference*.

## 1. Narrative psychology: why agency-centered framing changes reader response

**Narrative identity and the redemptive sequence.** Dan McAdams defines
narrative identity as the internalized, evolving life story a person builds to
give life unity and purpose. His coding scheme distinguishes a **redemption
sequence** (a scene moves from an emotionally negative state to a positive
outcome or self-attribution) from a **contamination sequence** (positivity is
overwhelmed and polluted by what follows). In the foundational 2001 study,
redemption sequences correlated positively with self-reported well-being;
contamination sequences predicted low well-being in midlife adults. McAdams and
McLean's 2013 review adds the operative finding: narrators who construct
stories featuring **personal agency and exploration** show higher mental health
and maturity.

- https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167201274008
- https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0963721413475622

**Implication.** The same two shapes are available for any historical chapter.
A chapter that ends on destruction is a contamination sequence. A chapter that
ends on what the survivors built, sued for, and testified to, without softening
what happened, is a redemptive sequence. The difference is not accuracy; both
can be equally sourced. It is sequence and terminal emphasis. The redemptive
arc becomes a lie when it is imposed: the rule is *end on agency, not on
resolution*. Agency can be documented (a lawsuit filed, a school founded, a
route north taken, a name recorded) where resolution often cannot.

**Transportation.** Green and Brock (JPSP, 2000) established transportation,
absorption into a story via imagery, affect, and attentional focus, as the
mechanism by which narratives shift beliefs. Highly transported readers
counterargue less and "find fewer false notes." Transportation is produced by
concrete sensory detail and a followable protagonist, not by argument density.

- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11079236/

**Implication.** Persuasion in this genre runs through immersion. Sourcing
earns the right to be believed; scene earns the reader's attention in the
first place. Lead every chapter with a person in a place at a time, never with
a thesis.

**Restorative narrative.** Images & Voices of Hope (now The Peace Studio)
coined the term for stories that follow "a person or community through a
meaningful progression from despair to resilience": engage the trauma fully,
then move the storyline past *what happened* to *what's possible*. This is the
journalistic operationalization of McAdams's redemption sequence.

- https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2015/whats-restorative-narrative-a-qa-with-a-journalism-professor-whos-studying-it/
- https://thepeacestudio.org/essays-that-emerged-from-ivohs-restorative-narrative-retreat-summit/

**Hope as a cognitive structure, not a mood.** Snyder's hope theory: hope is
agency thinking (motivational energy toward a goal) plus pathways thinking
(perceived ability to route to it). Both are required; agency without pathways
is frustration, pathways without agency is inertia.

- https://www.mindtools.com/aov3izj/snyders-hope-theory/

**Implication, as a craft rule.** Whenever the chapter documents an obstacle,
it should also document *how someone routed around it*, at the level of
method. Not "they persevered," but "they held classes in the church basement at
night because the school board would not fund a building." Naming the mechanism
is what converts a reader from sympathy to efficacy.

**Pedagogy corroboration.** Learning for Justice's *Teaching Hard History*
framework reaches the same conclusion independently: center "the agency and
voices of those who were enslaved," because "hard history is not hopeless
history," and documented resistance shows power exercised inside powerless
circumstances.

- https://www.learningforjustice.org/sites/default/files/2018-05/TT-Teaching-Hard-History-Framework-WEB-February2018.pdf

## 2. Black rhetorical tradition: teachable techniques

**The sermonic substrate.** Geneva Smitherman, *Talkin and Testifyin* (Wayne
State UP, 1977), is the standard scholarly account: Black English is set apart
not only by structure and lexicon but by a rhetorical style with African
roots — call-response, tonal semantics, signification, the sermonic mode.

- https://wsupress.wayne.edu/9780814318058/

Named techniques, each with a one-line prose translation:

| Technique | Prose form |
|---|---|
| Call-and-response | Pose the reader's objection as a short question, then answer it. "Was it legal? It was legal." |
| Anaphora | Repeat an opening clause across sentences to build pressure. "They took the land. They took the ledger. They took the name off the deed." |
| Tonal semantics / cadence | Vary sentence length deliberately: two long, one short. The short one lands. |
| Testifying | Let a primary source speak in its own words before you characterize it. |
| Understatement after magnitude | Give the number, then stop. "Three hundred people. The city called it a riot." |
| Naming | Full names of ordinary people on first mention, every time. Anonymity is the injury being repaired. |

**The African American jeremiad.** David Howard-Pitney, *The Afro-American
Jeremiad* (Temple UP): a rhetoric of indignation that holds America to its own
stated covenant, used in pure form by Douglass, Du Bois, and King. Its move is
not *you wronged us* but *you promised this, here is the document, here is the
ledger against it*: prosecutorial rather than plaintive, which keeps moral
authority with the narrator. Warning: the jeremiad's native register is the
pulpit; on the page at length it becomes lecture.

- https://www.bibliovault.org/BV.book.epl?ISBN=9781592134151
- https://networks.h-net.org/node/2602/reviews/2828/johnson-howard-pitney-african-american-jeremiad-appeals-justice-america

**HBCU oratory.** Howard University runs the Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr.
Forensics Society, competing primarily in parliamentary debate, a format
weighted toward oratorical and analytical ability rather than evidence-dumping
(https://communications.howard.edu/programs-and-centers/debate-team). The
transferable trait: **arguments are built to be spoken and heard once.** The
reader should be able to state the chapter's claim after one pass, from
memory. (A Morehouse-specific debate-society history could not be verified in
this pass; treat any such claim as unverified.)

**Craft signatures in the lineage** (craft consensus, not empirical):

- Baldwin: the long periodic sentence with a hinge; moral claim delivered as
  observation, not accusation; second person that implicates without indicting.
- Morrison: refusal to explain the culture to an outside gaze; the reader is
  assumed to be inside.
- Coates: second person as address; the body as unit of measure.
- Wilkerson: one person's ordinary decision carried across many pages, with
  statistics placed only after the reader is attached to the person.
- Hannah-Jones: the reframing lede, an assumed-settled fact restated so it
  becomes a question.
- Clint Smith, *How the Word Is Passed*: place as chapter unit, narrator
  physically present, interviews carried with care.
  (https://www.npr.org/2021/06/02/1002027683/how-the-word-is-passed-teaches-the-importance-of-reckoning-with-history)
- Imani Perry: the essayistic braid, personal register and archival register
  alternating without announcement.

## 3. Stage gravitas, translated to narration

**August Wilson's own account** (Paris Review interview, 1999; the four B's:
Bearden, Baraka, Borges, the blues): from Borges, "that you can be specific as
to a time and place and culture and still have the work resonate with the
universal themes"; from Bearden, that "the fullness and richness of everyday
life can be rendered without compromise or sentimentality."

- https://www.alleytheatre.org/august-wilsons-four-bs-the-art-that-inspired-the-american-century-cycle/

What produces gravitas in performance, translated (craft consensus):

1. **Restraint before the peak.** Never signal that a devastating fact is
   coming. Deliver it in the plainest available sentence.
2. **The pause.** On the page: a paragraph break, a one-sentence paragraph, or
   a full stop where a comma was expected.
3. **Moral authority through specificity, not volume.** The narrator never
   says "unimaginable"; the narrator itemizes.
4. **Earned emotion.** Adjectives before evidence read as manipulation; the
   same adjective after reads as verdict.
5. **Low, level register.** Gravitas is the absence of strain. The narrator is
   never surprised by the material and never performs outrage; the reader is
   left to supply it. This is the most reliable technical defense against
   bitterness or lecture.

## 4. How strong editorial brands codify voice

**The Marshall Project's Language Project** is the model worth imitating: not
a tone document but a word-level substitution table with published reasoning,
built on original research showing terms like "felon" and "inmate" were
experienced as stigmatizing. It moved the AP Stylebook.

- https://themarshallproject.org/2021/04/12/the-language-project
- https://www.themarshallproject.org/records/10631-person-first-language

Transferable structure: principle → preferred/avoided term pair → one-line
rationale. The rationale is what lets a writer generalize to cases the table
does not cover.

**Voice-attribute pattern** (e.g. Mailchimp's content style guide): a small
number of named attributes, each as *we are X, but we are not Y*. The "but
not" half carries the information; "confident" alone is not actionable,
"confident, but not arrogant" is.

## Known risk

An agent given "hope and spirit" will write inspirational filler. The defense
in the voice bible is that the agency-ending and pathways rules are *evidence
requirements*, not tone requirements: an agency ending must cite a source, or
the chapter is not done.
