You are here

Start where the color line became physical: Eugene Williams, teenager, 29th Street Beach, Chicago, 1919. The beats below walk policy, practice, lived place, measurement, and what the record still cannot close.

  1. 01 · United States (Chicago classroom)

    Before the maps: a color line walked into federal credit

    On July 27, 1919, Eugene Williams drifted across an invisible color line in Lake Michigan. A week of mob violence followed on Chicago's South Side. Great Migration families arriving on the South Side met private covenants, blockbusting, and the Chicago Race Riot of 1919, a week of mob violence that made the color line physical. Private real estate wrote those rules first. New Deal credit then made them federal. The Home Owners' Loan Act of 1933 created the Corporation that later graded neighborhoods; surveyors put race into the risk language of those sheets. The National Housing Act of 1934 created the Federal Housing Administration, whose underwriting manual rewarded same-race occupancy and restrictive covenants. Rothstein, Massey and Denton, Banaji, Fiske and Massey: the gated claim is about that underwriting system as a national machine, not one map causing every later loan. Chicago is the classroom where we learn to read the pattern.

    Law · 1933

    Home Owners' Loan Act

    Created the Home Owners' Loan Corporation to refinance distressed mortgages. The Corporation's later residential security maps left a federal paper trail of neighborhood risk grades that often named race as a credit factor.

    Law · 1934

    National Housing Act

    Created the Federal Housing Administration to insure private mortgages. Underwriting manuals of the late 1930s treated racial occupancy change and missing restrictive covenants as risks to mortgage security.

    On the desk National Archives Record Group 195 (Home Owners' Loan Corporation / Federal Home Loan Bank Board); Federal Housing Administration Underwriting Manual 1938 (FRASER); Rothstein, The Color of Law (2017); Massey and Denton, American Apartheid (1993); Banaji, Fiske and Massey (2021).

  2. 02 · Chicago HOLC survey

    Spread the Chicago sheets: most of the city is C or D

    Now open the maps. A direct recount of the Chicago Home Owners' Loan Corporation inventory yields 703 features: 683 normalized A-D grades (A 49, B 160, C 327, D 147) plus 20 ungraded commercial or industrial features. The area descriptions say the quiet part out loud about race and risk. What they will not give you is how many Black Chicagoans lived inside each grade. That missing count is why we refuse to pretend a polygon is a census.

    On the desk Mapping Inequality Chicago extract (University of Richmond DSL); National Archives Record Group 195; Hillier (2003). Gap: population-by-grade remains insufficient evidence. Polygons stay cite-only (CC BY-NC-SA).

  3. 03 · Cook County · national wealth for scale

    After credit learned the color line: ownership, denial, burden, wealth

    Leave the 1930s sheets on the desk and walk the county forward. In Cook County, NHGIS tenure shows Black homeownership rising from 37.1% in 1990 to 42.0% in 2000 and 41.2% in 2010, while the White rate stayed about 25 to 27 points higher. When NHGIS ends, the American Community Survey for 2020-2024 continues the same county ownership question on a later catalog; we name that seam instead of pretending the methods are identical. Home Mortgage Disclosure Act county aggregates for 2018 and 2023 still show a Black-White denial-rate gap (18.7 then 16.9 points). HUD CHAS for 2017-2021 shows a 29.5-point cost-burden gap. National Survey of Consumer Finances medians for 1989, 2010, and 2022 sit beside the metro reading for wealth scale, not as a county substitute. The Fair Housing Act of 1968 banned discrimination in the sale, rental, and financing of housing; the Community Reinvestment Act of 1977 pressed banks to meet credit needs in the communities they serve. Those statutes sit on the timeline as policy context; they do not turn later instrument gaps into automatic proof of one map's causal reach.

    Law · 1968

    Fair Housing Act

    Title VIII of the Civil Rights Act of 1968 banned discrimination in the sale, rental, and financing of housing based on race, color, religion, or national origin (later amendments added sex, disability, and familial status).

    Law · 1977

    Community Reinvestment Act

    Directed federal bank regulators to encourage depository institutions to meet the credit needs of the communities they serve, including low- and moderate-income neighborhoods, after decades of documented lending withdrawal.

    On the desk IPUMS NHGIS / Census tenure (1990-2010); American Community Survey 2020-2024 (Cook); FFIEC Home Mortgage Disclosure Act county aggregates (2018, 2023); HUD CHAS Table 9 (2017-2021); Federal Reserve Survey of Consumer Finances medians (1989, 2010, 2022 dollars).

  4. 04 · Bronzeville · county tape measure

    Bronzeville on the map; the county still holds the tape measure

    Now name the neighborhood. Bronzeville is Chicago's Black Metropolis after 1916 along State Street and South Parkway: the Defender, Overton Hygienic, Supreme Life, the Wabash Avenue YMCA. Robert S. Abbott built the Chicago Defender into a national voice from this corridor. Drake and Cayton gave the district its lasting name in 1945. The same South Side geography that survived the 1919 riot later sat under Home Owners' Loan Corporation grades and Federal Housing Administration underwriting. Bind the place; then open the Cook County instruments that already walk homeownership across 1990, 2000, 2010, and the American Community Survey handoff, plus Home Mortgage Disclosure Act denial years 2018 and 2023. Those series still resolve only to the county. They cannot find the same households inside a particular Corporation polygon. Local story, county tape measure, until tract linkage closes.

Gap: tract-level household linkage still insufficient.
